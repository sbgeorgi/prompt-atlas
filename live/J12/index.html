The studio

A room built around the slow parts of making.

Most of pottery is waiting: for clay to firm, for a pot to dry, for a kiln to cool. We built the studio so that waiting is pleasant. Long benches, a stove for tea, shelves where unfinished work can rest without being rushed.

Members share one clay body, mixed in house from local stoneware and fire-clay, so pots from every shelf can go in the same firing.

Read about our clay

  • Local stoneware

    Dug twenty miles upriver, aged in tubs for a season before it reaches a wheel.

  • Open studio hours

    Members work when they like, Tuesday to Sunday, with a teacher on the floor most evenings.

  • Wood-assisted kiln

    Fired every second Friday. Ash settles on the shoulders of each pot and no two come out alike.

The process

From a lump on the wheel to something you drink from.

Every course follows the same four stages. You will make fewer pots than you expect and keep more of them than you fear.

  1. 01

    Center and open

    Weeks one and two are about the clay. Wedge it, center it, open a small cylinder. Most people collapse a dozen of them. That is the lesson.

  2. 02

    Pull and shape

    Lift the walls with your hands and a sponge. Learn to read thickness through your fingertips and to stop before the pot tells you to.

  3. 03

    Trim and dry

    When the pot is leather hard we turn it over and trim the foot. Then it rests under paper for a few days while the water leaves slowly.

  4. 04

    Glaze and fire

    Dip, pour or brush from our short palette of ash, iron and celadon glazes. Pots go into the Friday firing and come out transformed.

Questions

Before you come in.

Do I need any experience?

None. First Wheel assumes you have never touched clay. Bring clothes that can get dirty and keep your nails short.

When do I get my finished pots?

About three weeks after you make them. Pots need to dry, then go through a bisque firing, a glaze and a final firing. We email when yours are ready.

Are the glazes food safe?

Every glaze on our palette is formulated and tested for tableware. We label any that are decorative only, mostly the ash runs on outdoor pieces.

Can I cancel or move a class?

You can move to another session up to a week before it starts. After that we offer a credit toward a later course.
