Feature · The method

A ledger you can read from the top of the page to the bottom.

Most accounting software is built to be glanced at. The Daybook is built to be read. Each entry occupies a line, each heading a section, each month a page, and the totals sit where a reader expects to find them: at the foot, beneath a heavy rule.

Because the layout never changes, patterns show themselves. A rising heading is visible without a chart. A strange entry stands out because everything near it is ordinary.

Behind the page sits a strict double-entry engine, so every figure balances. You never see the machinery unless you ask for it, and when you do, it is a table of debits and credits, not a maze of tabs.

Studios keep their ledgers open all year. At close, the Daybook sets the year in order, carries the balances forward, and starts a clean sheet.

Section 04Letters from studios
Questions

Asked and answered.

Can I leave and take my books with me?

Yes. Export every entry as a plain text file and an ordinary spreadsheet at any time, on any plan.

Does it touch my money?

No. Bank connections are read-only, so the Daybook can see transactions but cannot move funds.

Do I need to know accounting?

No. You name headings and confirm entries. The double-entry bookkeeping happens beneath the page.

What happens at the end of the year?

One button closes the year, prints the full ledger, carries balances forward and opens a clean sheet.
