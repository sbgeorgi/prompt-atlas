Small habits that keep work honest

Draw it first

A rough line beats a paragraph. Start every idea as a quick sketch and let words follow it.

fast > pretty

Note in the margin

Comments live beside the thing they are about, in blue, so nobody hunts through a thread to find the point.

Fix in red

A correction is a mark on the page, not a new version. Cross it out, write the better answer, and move on.

history stays visible

Keep what works

Highlight the choice you are keeping. The yellow stays after the discussion is over.

Tape in references

Photos, screenshots and swatches sit on the sheet as taped scraps, right next to the sketch they inspired.

Pick it up later

Open the sheet next week and it reads exactly as you left it. No catch-up call, no recap email.

the loop

From scribble to settled

  1. 1

    Sketch

    Drop a rough idea on the sheet. Boxes, arrows and bad handwriting all welcome.

  2. 2

    Note

    Teammates answer in blue ink, right on the part they mean. One thread per spot.

  3. 3

    Correct

    Cross out what is wrong and write the fix in red. The old line stays legible.

  4. 4

    Keep

    Highlight the winner in yellow. That is the decision, and it is easy to find again.

margin questions

Things people ask

Do I need to draw well?
No. Wobbly boxes and arrows are the point. The sheet is for thinking, not for presenting.
Can the whole team edit at once?
Yes. Everyone gets their own ink colour, and the page keeps every note exactly where it was written.
What happens to old sheets?
They stay in a stack you can flip through. Nothing is overwritten, crossed-out lines remain readable.
